# Orbital presentation carousel (local first iteration)

The shared carousel style rules for this presentation and the independent fragmentation carousel are in `../../../../../Talks/CAROUSEL_STYLE_FAMILY.md`. The two presentations keep separate content, assets, servers, and comment stores.

This independent presentation lives at `presentation/` and does not alter the Episodes of Concern library. Start `python3 presentation/server.py` from the repository root, then open:

- Review and design: `http://127.0.0.1:8772/presentation/?mode=review`
- Clean presentation: `http://127.0.0.1:8772/presentation/?mode=present`

The review server saves edited presets, chapter overrides, opening artwork settings, grid-snapped component positions, component visibility, and camera views in `presentation/config.json`. It saves spot comments and their open or archived state in `presentation/comments.json`. Both are local and ignored by Git. `defaults.json` is the shared starting configuration. Right Arrow, Page Down, and Space advance one slide or teaching beat; Left Arrow and Page Up go back. The **Within slide** menu jumps directly to a beat; the small Previous and Next controls provide a visible alternative. Clicking a noninteractive slide also advances. Orbital canvases, controls, and the design inspector remain independently clickable. The playback button pauses or resumes a technical chapter. Choose a chapter in the top bar, adjust parameters in Scenario design, then save the preset, chapter configuration, or camera for the current teaching beat. Drag the handles on the legend, metrics, or side panel to place them on a 2.5% grid; positions save per chapter. The clean view reads the same saved configuration.

## Simulation model

The opening artwork borrows the invitation studio's two-body LEO target and chaser, sampling each point of the animated trail in the target's changing radial, along-track, cross-track frame. The presentation runs continuously: when a chaser leaves the camera view, it fades out and the next synthetic pass fades in. This handoff is a presentation device, not a continuous trajectory of one object. The editor adjusts satellite dots, animation speed, trail length and thickness, reference-orbit thickness, Earth starting longitude, and border color, opacity, and thickness. Earth turns in the changing local frame. Optional additional chasers are separate propagated orbits with different initial phase. The title-section corner art reuses this same orbital model. The chapter scenes use **synthetic** two-line element sets constructed from the inspector's orbital parameters. The TLE lesson permits non-GEO orbits as long as perigee stays above 160 km; the other presets begin at GEO. Their initial states and chart samples come from the bundled SGP4 propagator. Chapter 1's final beat adds an illustrative 180 m/s tangential impulse to Object A near apogee; its post-burn path is propagated continuously from the burn state, the previous orbit remains drawn, and a synthetic post-burn element set is displayed. Other optional maneuver sequences apply instantaneous tangential impulses to Object B and coast under a two-body universal-variable solution. These controls describe impulses and time between them; they do not model continuous thrust or a finite-duration burn.

This is an **illustrative** design model. It does not include measured covariance, observations, a fitted orbit, real station-keeping commands, continuous thrust, or full perturbation consistency between the post-burn two-body arc and the SGP4 baseline. Long propagation after a burn can accumulate model differences. The uncertainty ellipsoids are explicitly hypothetical. Their long axes follow each object's along-track direction; their sizes grow by a simple 18% per day from the synthetic TLE epoch. This rate is a visual teaching heuristic, not a covariance estimate derived from a paper or calibrated to these TLEs. Public TLEs do not include covariance; [CelesTrak's conjunction assessment](https://celestrak.org/publications/aas/05-124/6USR-SSW-S2.2.pdf) and [NASA's Covariance Realism Handbook](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180003657/downloads/20180003657.pdf) explain why calibrated uncertainty requires additional data and assessment. Relative speed is the magnitude of the inertial velocity difference; range rate is the derivative of separation distance. Dwell time is summed from sampled midpoint intervals satisfying both user-set thresholds, so it is approximate and depends on chart resolution. No simulation establishes intent.

## Review conventions

`✚ Spot comment` lets a reviewer click a slide, scene, chart, card, playback bar, or inspector control. Comments store chapter, component, location, and simulation time. Completed comments are hidden from the default list; **Show archived** reveals them without changing the fresh review view. These comments are separate from the Dynamic Table and website review comment files.

The latest carousel round is archived in `comments-2026-11-22-round-6-archived.json`; the active review shows zero old comments. The local project-page slide uses the review server's `/api/proximity-project` route because the lab site permits framing only from the same origin. The side-event concept note contains **four** discussion questions; the dedicated question slide reproduces all four verbatim. Opening target elements, chaser relative elements, camera, artwork, and chapter settings remain saved in the local `config.json`.

The close-approach statement slide uses three draft excerpts supplied by the project lead. The U.S. Raymond excerpt was checked against [CBS News, 12 February 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/russian-satellites-fly-near-us-government-satellite-activity-unusual-disturbing-space-command/). The UK and China excerpts point to UN Geneva recordings in `app.js`; the Chinese satellite designations in the supplied English transcription could not be independently confirmed from the recording and are explicitly labeled unverified on the slide. The shadowing excerpts retain their UN recording references in `app.js` for later review.
